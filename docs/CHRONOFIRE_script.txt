In recent fire seasons, severe wildfires have shattered records from South Asia’s Himalayan forests to Canada and Australia. But when climate scientists analyzed NASA satellite data, they uncovered a baffling paradox: recorded fire hotspots had suddenly multiplied by over four hundred percent.

Was the planet burning four times faster overnight? Or was satellite technology deceiving us?

For NASA Space Apps 2026 Challenge 09, we present CHRONOFIRE: harmonizing twenty-six years of conflicting Earth observations into one synchronized, indisputable climate truth.

For two decades, NASA’s MODIS sensor on Terra and Aqua provided our one-kilometer fire baseline. But in 2012, NASA launched VIIRS with three-hundred-and-seventy-five-meter optics. Because VIIRS has seven times higher pixel density, a single fire registers as multiple separate hot pixels.

The result? Over one hundred and sixty-eight months of overlap, raw VIIRS records an average of four point three eight times more hotspots than MODIS for the exact same fires. Comparing raw data distorts long-term climate models, unless we fix the sensor physics.

To solve this, we built CHRONOFIRE’s three-stage harmonization engine.

First, we project all hotspots onto a uniform zero point zero five degree spatial grid, roughly five point five kilometers.

Second, we collapse multiple intra-day detections inside each cell into a single discrete Cell-Day Fire Event, instantly removing over seventy-six percent of VIIRS clustering.

Third, calibrated across fourteen years of overlap from 2012 to 2025, our transfer function eliminates ninety-five point one percent of Root Mean Square Error, restoring true scientific parity.

To handle forty-one gigabytes of raw NASA FIRMS archives, we built an embedded Duck-D-B analytical engine. Using columnar Parquet, CHRONOFIRE queries sixty-seven million hotspots across Asia in under one hundred and fifty milliseconds, one hundred percent locally, with zero cloud fees and zero API keys.

This is the live CHRONOFIRE platform. Built with React and Leaflet, it delivers full-screen Pan-Asian coverage across fourteen pre-indexed nations.

Researchers can draw custom bounding boxes anywhere on the map to get instant spatial telemetry and export clean CSV datasets, powered entirely by free Open Street Map tiles.

Here is the core deliverable: the twenty-six-year Burning Activity Calendar. Every month from 2000 to 2025 is benchmarked against its historical median. Disaster managers can immediately distinguish dangerous climate anomalies from harmless seasonal crop burning.

CHRONOFIRE turns twenty-six years of fragmented satellite readings into an open, unified climate baseline, ready for real-time NOAA twenty-one feeds.
